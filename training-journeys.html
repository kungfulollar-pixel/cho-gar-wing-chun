Kung Fu at its Roots

Training Journeys

Kung Fu is passed down within families. That is why Sifu Nils Ring travels to Asia regularly, trains with his teachers there and keeps the bond with the Ip Man Family and the Cho Family alive. Here you will find impressions from our journeys.

January and February 2025

Training Journey to China & Singapore

In early 2025 we spent around four weeks in Asia. The focus was on training with the Cho Family and experiencing Kung Fu where it is at home: in the streets, at festivals and within the family.

A special moment of the journey: in February 2025, Sifu Nils Ring was accepted into the Cho Family in a tea ceremony. He is the only person in Germany entitled to represent the Cho Family.

The video shows how naturally Kung Fu is part of everyday life in China: even the youngest show their skills in front of an audience. More photos and videos from this journey will follow.

Lunar New Year 2025

A Festival Full of Kung Fu

The Spring Festival is the biggest celebration of the year in China. As a member of the Cho Family, Sifu Nils Ring celebrates it with his Kung Fu family: with a festive banquet, lion dance, firecrackers and demonstrations. Here, Kung Fu is not a club sport but part of life and of the community.

Together at One Table

For the New Year festival the whole family comes together. Young and old sit side by side at round tables dressed in red, eating, celebrating and talking. The table is richly laid, because nothing should be lacking in the new year.

The Lions Awaken

Dozens of lions, waving flags and fireworks over the square. In southern China the lion dance has always been part of Kung Fu: underneath the heads are martial artists who need a solid stance, strength and plenty of stamina.

Firecrackers Against Misfortune

According to old custom, the noise of the firecrackers drives away evil spirits and makes room for a good new year. Smoke, thunder and sparks: in the end the whole square is covered in scraps of red paper.

The Next Generation Shows Its Skills

On the festival square, children and teenagers perform weapon forms, accompanied by the lions. A beautiful picture of how Kung Fu in China is passed on from generation to generation.
